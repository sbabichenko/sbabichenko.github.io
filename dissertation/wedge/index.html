The Price of Changing Someone’s Mind

Two coworkers share an office and one thermostat. One likes it cold. The other likes it warm. Neither can see the dial from their desk. They only feel the room, and not very well.

So one nudges it down, the other nudges it up, and they keep at it all day. The room ends up where it started. The heating bill does not.

Every nudge is also a message. Turn the dial down, and the other feels the chill and turns it back. The better they feel the room, the less a nudge gets you, and the less you both nudge.

What it is worth to move an opponent’s beliefs. Two of the drawings are solved as you read, by the same solver as the explorer, on the two-player tracking game of Chapter 1. Loading the solver…

One controller, one hidden state. Estimate the state, then act on the estimate exactly as you would on the state itself. How you learn and how you act are separate problems.

The separation principle. Simple, and its intuition has held back the multi-player version for decades.

Add a second player who watches the same state through a signal of their own. Now every push you give the state also shows up in what they see.

They revise their forecast, change what they do, and that moves the state again.

Optimal control prices a move of the state with a shadow price: how much better the future looks if the state is a little higher now. The best action sets the cost of effort against that price.

In a game there is a second thing a push moves: the other player’s noise-state. The information wedge is its price, the value of changing an opponent’s beliefs.

Player 1’s first-order condition at mid-horizon, split into its physical part and the wedge, for each shock it responds to.

The physical part is what the condition would be if nobody reacted to player 1’s deviation. The wedge is the rest: player 2 revising their forecast.

Let both players see more clearly. Every push is now answered, so each pushes less, and both pay less. The state never moves: the effort only holds it where it is.

Blind them both and nobody answers anybody: the wedge vanishes, and they push as if nobody were watching.
