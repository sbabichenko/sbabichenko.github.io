A simpler form of the condition · added after the dissertation

Setting. Stack the increments of \(W\) on \([0,T]\), past and future, state shocks and signal noise alike, into one standard normal vector \(\varepsilon\sim N(0,I)\). Take the tracking case with no linear part (\(k=0\)), so that the cost is a quadratic form, \(\mathcal C^i=c_0+\tfrac12\,\varepsilon^\top K\varepsilon\), with \(K\) the kernel (1.B.28). Assume \(K\) is positive semidefinite and \(\theta_i\lambda_{\max}(K)<1\).

What player \(i\) has observed by time \(t\) is a set of linear combinations of \(\varepsilon\). Let \(P_t\) be the projection matrix onto their span. Then

\[\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid\mathcal F_t^i]=P_t\,\varepsilon,\]

\[\operatorname{Var}(\varepsilon\mid\mathcal F_t^i)=I-P_t\eqqcolon\Sigma_t .\]

Let \(H^{X,i}_t=\bar H^{X,i}_t+\int_0^TH^{X,i}_t(u)\,dW_u\) be player \(i\)'s state price, the adjoint of Theorem 1.8, and let

\[M_t=G^{DD,i}(t)\,D^i_t+B^X_i(t)^\top H^{X,i}_t\]

be the marginal cost of its control at \(t\): the control's own cost plus the state price, carried through \(B^X_i\) (in the two-player tracking game, \(2r_iD^i_t+H^{X,i}_t\)). This is the bracket of (1.B.10), and the first-order condition sets its conditional mean to zero. Under a linear profile \(M_t\) is a linear function of the shocks, so it can be written with a fixed vector \(a_t\) of loadings,

\[M_t=a_t^\top\varepsilon .\]

(For a vector control, take one \(a_t\) per component.) The risk-neutral condition then reads \(\mathbb E[M_t\mid\mathcal F_t^i]=a_t^\top P_t\,\varepsilon=0\) for every \(\varepsilon\), that is, \(P_ta_t=0\): the loadings have no component along what player \(i\) has observed.

Proposition. For each \(t\), the following are equivalent:

\[\text{(a)}\quad P_t\,(I-\theta_iK)^{-1}a_t=0,\]

\[\text{(b)}\quad P_t\,(I-\theta_iK\Sigma_t)^{-1}a_t=0 .\]

Condition (b) is the stationarity condition of Theorem 1.18: it evaluates \(a_t\) at \((I-\theta_i\Sigma_tK)^{-1}m\) for every \(m\) in the range of \(P_t\), and \(\Sigma_t\) and \(K\) are symmetric. Condition (a) is the same condition with \(\Sigma_t\) replaced by \(I\): stationarity when the whole game is judged from the start, under the tilted law \(\varepsilon\sim N\bigl(0,(I-\theta_iK)^{-1}\bigr)\).

Proof. Both inverses exist, because \(0\preceq\Sigma_t\preceq I\). The two matrices agree on vectors orthogonal to everything observed: if \(P_tv=0\), then \(\Sigma_tv=v\), and so

\[(I-\theta_iK\Sigma_t)\,v=(I-\theta_iK)\,v .\qquad(\ast)\]

Suppose (a), and set \(u=(I-\theta_iK)^{-1}a_t\). Then \(P_tu=0\), so \((\ast)\) gives \((I-\theta_iK\Sigma_t)\,u=a_t\). Hence \((I-\theta_iK\Sigma_t)^{-1}a_t=u\), and (b) holds.

Conversely, suppose (b), and set \(v=(I-\theta_iK\Sigma_t)^{-1}a_t\). Then \(P_tv=0\), so \((\ast)\) gives \((I-\theta_iK)\,v=a_t\). Hence \((I-\theta_iK)^{-1}a_t=v\), and (a) holds. \(\square\)

Remark. Both conditions say \(\mathbb E^{Q}[M_t\mid\mathcal F_t^i]=0\), computed two ways: (a) conditions the tilted law \(Q\) on \(\mathcal F_t^i\), (b) tilts the conditional law. By Bayes' rule these are the same law, since \(Q(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_t^i)\propto e^{\theta_i\mathcal C^i}P(\cdot\mid\mathcal F_t^i)\).

The matrix in (a) is the same for every \(t\) and needs no conditional variance. Since

\[(I-\theta_iK)^{-1}=I+\theta_iK(I-\theta_iK)^{-1},\]

condition (a) is the risk-neutral condition \(P_ta_t=0\) plus one correction, and it is through this correction that the unobserved part of \(a_t\), including its future part, enters. The entropic cost itself is finite exactly when \(I-\theta_iK\) is positive definite.
