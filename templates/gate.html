A mesh that knows when to stop

Somewhere on this square the odds of a coin change. A few thousand sites each flip their own coin a handful of times. The estimator cuts a mesh, of right triangles or of rectangles, only where a false-discovery gate says the flips justify a cut, and it stops when no candidate passes.

  • my two C++ estimators, compiled to WebAssembly
  • same meshes as the native builds, to 1e-11
  • runs in your browser, in under a second
Ready

The gate, round by round

Each round scores every candidate vertex (the midpoints of the mesh's edges) by how much it would improve the fit. If no candidate mattered, the scores would look like draws from one bell curve, the null. The gate fits the density of all the scores, reads the null's centre and spread off the middle of it, estimates the share π0 that are noise, and admits the candidates that stand out from that null at a controlled false-discovery rate. When the scores have no central peak to read a null from, it falls back to the textbook one. The pool variance is the spread of the coins around the surface, fitted alongside it: as the mesh learns the odds, what it cannot explain shrinks toward the coins' true spread.

Try giving the two hills more flips and watch the mesh fill in around them; pick “nothing at all”, which it leaves uncut at any number of flips; pick “your drawing” and paint on the odds (drag to raise them, tick “lower” or hold shift to lower them).

The estimators are triangular-decision-mesh and rectangular-decision-mesh, compiled from their C++ with Emscripten. Their dense linear algebra, which natively calls LAPACK, runs on Eigen here; nothing else is changed, and on my test designs the browser builds reproduce the native meshes to 1e-11. The two share the gate and the pool model and differ in geometry: right triangles cut only through their hypotenuse, with a flat fit in each; rectangles cut across either axis, with a bilinear fit in each. Half the sites are held out: the mesh never sees them, and the deviance card scores the fit on them. The coins are drawn on this page from the odds you pick, with a random nudge per site (standard deviation 0.25 on the log-odds).

The growing-mesh page grows the same geometries by a simple greedy rule with no gate: freeform triangles, right triangles, rectangles and a tree, grown greedily side by side. The right triangles there are this estimator's geometry.
