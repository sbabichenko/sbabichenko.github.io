Decision Mesh

A decision tree cuts the plane into boxes and predicts a constant in each, so its fit is a staircase. A decision mesh cuts it into triangles and predicts linearly across each, with heights shared at the corners, so its fit is continuous. Press play and watch both grow on the same noisy data, piece for piece.

  • runs in your browser
  • JavaScript port of DecisionMesh.py, matching it to 1e-12
  • a few milliseconds a step
How it grows, in four sentences

Every edge of the mesh carries a candidate vertex at its midpoint; activating it splits the one or two triangles on that edge through their opposite corners, and every active vertex can also have its height refitted. Each candidate knows the least-squares height it would take with every other height held fixed, and how much squared error that would save. Each step takes the candidate with the largest saving, unless the split would make a triangle more than the aspect-ratio limit times longer than it is wide; with probability ε it instead splits the longest edge of a triangle drawn with weight area × points, which lets it get past places where no single split helps. The tree beside it is ordinary best-first CART, grown to as many leaves as the mesh has triangles.

Error against the truth

Root-mean-square distance from the true surface over the square, as the pieces grow; the dashed line is the noise σ. On smooth surfaces (the saddle, the hills, the cliff) the mesh pulls ahead once it has a couple of hundred pieces. The tree does better on the disk, whose edge is a jump, on the ripples, whose peaks line up with the axes, and at the very start, when a few boxes beat a few triangles. Late in a run a thin triangle holding a handful of points can take an extreme height: those are the jumps in the mesh's curve. Setting a minimum of 3 points per triangle (0 is the algorithm as published) removes them and leaves the best error about where it was. "Refresh fits" refits the candidate splits around a vertex whose height has just been refitted, which the published algorithm leaves stale. With both on, on the default data, the best error is 2 to 17% below the published algorithm's on five of the six surfaces; the ripples come out 3% worse.

The algorithm is my Decision Mesh; this page runs a line-for-line JavaScript port whose squared error matches the Python's to 1e-12 over the first 400 steps (with ε = 0). Data, splits and pictures are all computed on this page.
